THE 2024 GOOGLE API LEAK
TYPE: EVIDENCE CHAPTER EVENT DATE: March–May 2024 SCOPE: selected fields traced to version 0.4.0 AUTHENTICITY: confirmed by Google editorial revision 2026-09-19

Analysts published accounts of internal Google Content Warehouse documentation in May 2024. Google subsequently acknowledged the documents, while warning against decontextualized conclusions. This chapter distinguishes the reported fields from analyst interpretation and from separate public court evidence. The selected field audit below now pins a public package, checksum, module and line context. It does not claim an exhaustive module/attribute census. [1][2][3]

Evidence hierarchy: confirmed Google / primary documentation leak-indicated present in the leaked docs interpretation researcher inference experimental independent test practitioner field observation anecdotal disputed

1 · What the leak is

The documents are automatically-generated reference material for an internal Google API called the Content Warehouse. They list modules, features, and attributes (field names, types, and short descriptions) that Google's systems use to represent documents, links, users, and search interactions. They were briefly exposed in a public code repository, discovered, and shared with SEO analyst Mike King (iPullRank) and marketer Rand Fishkin (SparkToro), who published them in late May 2024. leak-indicated[1][2]

GOOGLE CONFIRMED THE DOCUMENTS ARE REAL

This is the crucial point that makes the leak citable. Google confirmed to journalists that the documentation is authentic, while cautioning against "making inaccurate assumptions" because the material may be out of context, outdated, or incomplete. confirmed[3] Authenticity of the documents does not independently verify this archive’s transcription of every field or its current use in ranking.

3 · What researchers infer (and what is disputed)

Everything in this section is interpretation. It is the analysts' reading of the fields above, not Google policy, and some of it is contested even within the SEO community.[4]

  • Clicks influence ranking via NavBoost. Public 2023 antitrust testimony separately describes NavBoost’s use of click information. The meaning and current production use of each leaked field remain a different question. [5]
  • Links are gated into quality tiers by traffic. King reads the tiered link index as meaning links on pages with no clicks are shunted to a low-quality tier and effectively ignored. This is the interpretation the PBN chapter leans on, always as inference. interpretation
  • A site-wide authority score exists. The presence of siteAuthority is read as contradicting Google's public "we don't have domain authority" messaging, though its weight in ranking is unknown. interpretation
  • New sites are dampened ("sandbox"). Age fields are read as supporting a long-suspected new-site suppression period. interpretation
  • Anchor text is less central than assumed. A widely-cited but debated reading. disputed
WHAT THE LEAK DOES NOT PROVE

A stored attribute is not a confirmed ranking factor. The docs do not include weights, do not say which fields are live, and Google explicitly warned they may be outdated or lack context. confirmed[3] A ranking-use claim needs context and corroboration beyond the existence of a stored attribute.

4 · Why it matters for black hat SEO

The documents suggest research questions about click information, links and site-level signals. They do not demonstrate that purchased clicks, trafficless links or any other tactic succeeds. To evaluate a hypothesis, preserve the exact schema/module/version, quote its relevant context, identify the analyst’s inference, and compare it with independently measured outcomes. This archive supplies a versioned field trace, secondary analyses and proposed studies. It still lacks current production-use evidence or a causal validation of those ranking hypotheses.
